
## **Notable Policies in Europe:**

**Directive (EU) 2023/1791, Article 12 and Annex VII (recast Energy Efficiency Directive)**
- Member States must require owners and operators of data centres with installed IT power demand of at least 500 kW to make the information in Annex VII publicly available, except where covered by trade secret or confidentiality law.
- Operators report either directly to the European Database on Data Centres or to a national platform where their state requires it.
- The first report covering calendar year 2023 was due by 15 September 2024, and from 2025 the deadline is 15 May for the preceding year.
- Reported KPIs include PUE, Water Usage Effectiveness, Energy Reuse Factor and Renewable Energy Factor.
  
**Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2024/1364**
- Sets out the first phase of a common Union rating scheme for data centres, giving Article 12 operational content. 
- It establishes the reporting scheme and lists the data points to be collected 
- Relevant data includes a standard EU template covering surface area, installed power, energy consumption, capacity utilisation, waste heat and water consumption. 
- Individual KPIs and reported information are kept confidential, with Article 12(3) requiring the European database to be public only at aggregated level.

**Germany: Energy Efficiency Act (EnEfG), 2023**
- Applies to data centres with non-redundant installed electrical capacity of at least 300 kW. Facilities operational before 1 July 2026 must reach an annual-average PUE of ≤1.5 from 1 July 2027 and ≤1.3 from 1 July 2030;
- Those commissioned on or after 1 July 2026 must hit ≤1.2 from the outset. The 300 kW floor captures roughly 1,000 of Germany's 3,000-plus data centres. 
- New facilities face escalating waste heat reuse targets; 10% ERF from July 2026, 15% from 2027, 20% from 2028, with renewable electricity sourcing rising from 50% in 2024 to 100% from January 2027.
- Operators must run an ISO 50001 or EMAS energy management system by 1 July 2025, certified by 1 January 2026 for facilities ≥1 MW, and must publish and submit prescribed performance data annually by 31 March. Fines reach €100,000.
- Note: Reform Bills currently being considered, which would reduce waste heat requirements ( or make them non-binding) and potentially increase compliance windows.

**Netherlands: National Restriction on Hyperscale Data Centres, 2022**
- The preliminary decision prohibited changing the use of land or buildings to allow a hyperscale data centre until new national siting conditions were set, applying nationwide except in Hollands Kroon and Het Hogeland, which the National Environmental Vision designates as preferred areas. 
- "Hyperscale" is defined as more than 10 hectares with a 70 MW electrical connected load, including functionally supporting activities on the same site; the rules bite only on new facilities, not those already built or permitted. 
- The stated grounds were spatial and energy-related rather than efficiency-based: hyperscale facilities take up a lot of space and consume a disproportionate share of available renewable energy.

**Ireland: CRU Decision CRU/2025/236, Large Energy Users Connection Policy**
- For facilities with maximum import capacity above 10 MVA, assessment criteria include the ability to provide new onsite or proximate dispatchable generation, with a de minimis MIC threshold of under 1 MVA. 
- Applicants must meet at least 80% of annual demand with additional renewable projects generating in the Republic of Ireland, and generation already contracted elsewhere does not count toward additionality. 
- System operators may reduce MIC or shift a connection to non-firm status where the associated generation or storage misses performance and availability standards, and may terminate applications outright

**France: Loi n° 2025-391 du 30 avril 2025 (loi DDADUE), Article 25, creating Code de l'énergie, Chapitre VI "La performance énergétique des centres de données" (arts. L. 236-1 to L. 236-3)**
- Article L. 236-1 defines a data centre as a structure or group of structures used to host, connect and operate computer systems and related equipment for storage, processing or distribution of data, and applies the chapter to all data centres, expressly including those hosted by companies, banks and research centres, so the French threshold of 500 kW catches enterprise facilities where organisations host their own servers, not just colocation. Defence and civil-protection facilities are carved out. 
- Article L. 236-2 requires data centres with installed capacity of 1 MW or more to valorise the waste heat they produce, with implementing conditions and derogations, including the case in Article 26(6) of Directive 2023/1791; set by decree in Conseil d'État. This obligation took effect on 1 October 2025; L. 236-1 applied from 3 May 2025.
- Article L. 236-3 creates the sanction: the administrative authority can publicly issue a formal notice giving up to one year to comply, and on failure impose a fine of up to €50,000 per data centre. 
- The 2025 decree requires operators to transmit administrative, environmental and energy information to a digital platform and make the same information public, annually before 15 May for the preceding calendar year, with pro-rata obligations for facilities in service under a year.
- France also went beyond disclosure: the ministers for energy and environment set the general rules, technical prescriptions and siting arrangements applicable to the construction and operation of data centres, and the law inserted energy consumption into the environmental impact assessment provisions of the Code de l'environnement. 

**Portugal: Decreto-Lei n.º 84/2024, de 4 de novembro**
- It establishes the regime for monitoring and publishing information on data centre energy performance for facilities on national territory with installed IT power demand of at least 500 kW. 
- Covered operators must report the information in Annexes I and II of Delegated Regulation 2024/1364 to the Commission platform by 15 May each year, with the European database published in aggregated form. 
- The decree also requires operators to make the data publicly available on their own websites in a clear and easily accessible manner by 15 May annually, and Article 7 provides for administrative offence penalties. It additionally allocates competences among the public bodies involved.
- Useful as a "minimum transposition" case: unlike France or Germany, Portugal added no performance obligation, no waste heat duty and no siting rule. Pure Article 12 implementation.
- Note: Spain's draft royal decree, opened to public consultation on 27 August 2026, is described as covering the same publication procedure but going further, adding additionality and hourly-matching requirements. 

**United Kingdom: AI Growth Zones (AI Opportunities Action Plan, 2025; DSIT policy paper Delivering AI Growth Zones, December 2025)**
- DSIT launched the AI Growth Zones initiative and proposed regulations allowing individual UK data centre projects to be treated as Nationally Significant Infrastructure Projects for consenting purposes, with initial zones in Oxfordshire, South and North Wales and the North East.
- Facilities inside a designated zone can be handled as NSIPs, with government targeting a cut in average consenting time from 18 months to 12. 
- Government estimates a 500 MW data centre could save around £80 million a year on electricity bills and connect to the grid five years sooner under the proposed reforms, with energy cost discounts from April 2027. The discount rationale is regional: where generation exceeds the grid's ability to move it, data centres siting in those areas receive commensurate discounts on electricity costs. 
- On the grid side, the package manages speculative demand in the connections queue by prioritising credible developments, creates two new connection mechanisms, enables developers to build their own high-voltage infrastructure instead of waiting for network operators, and adds a Connections Accelerator Service.
- The revised National Planning Policy Framework published on 17 August 2026 strengthens planning support for AI Growth Zones and data centres, requiring planning strategy to recognise data-driven industries and placing greater emphasis on the strategic electricity infrastructure needed to support them.

### Recent and Upcoming Measures: 

- **Second delegated regulation - EU sustainability rating scheme and label**. Draft registered 26 March 2026, consultation closed 23 April, revised draft published 2 July. It supplements the EED and amends Delegated Regulation 2024/1364. From 15 August 2027 annually, the European database generates an electronic label for every reporting data centre, grading PUE and WUE A–G with a REF breakdown. Still a draft as of late August 2026.
  
- **Data Centre Energy Efficiency Package**. Comprises a report on data centre energy efficiency, the rating scheme delegated act, and a public consultation on minimum performance standards for new and existing EU data centres. The rating covers energy efficiency, water efficiency, clean energy use, waste heat reuse and flexibility.
  
- **Minimum performance standards study (DG ENER)**. A Commission-contracted study supporting the development of minimum performance standards for European data centres, aligned with existing reporting requirements and the rating scheme.

- **Cloud and AI Development Act (CADA)**. Proposed June 2026 in the Tech Sovereignty Package. Targets at least tripling EU data centre capacity within five to seven years and meeting EU business and public-administration needs by 2035, via site identification and simplified permitting for projects meeting sustainability and innovation criteria. Member States would designate acceleration areas; developers get a single point of information for permits. Adoption scheduled for end-2027.
